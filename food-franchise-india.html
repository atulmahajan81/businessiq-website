Food and beverage is the largest franchise category in India, and the one where international brands have found the fastest traction. Rising disposable income in metros and Tier-2 cities, combined with a young population that eats out frequently, has made F&B the default entry point for first-time franchise investors.

Choosing the right food franchise format

Kiosk formats (₹15 – 25 Lakhs)

The fastest route into F&B franchising. A kiosk needs 100–250 sq ft, runs with two or three staff, and can open within 6–10 weeks of signing. Because rent and payroll are low, break-even is typically faster than a full restaurant — but you are dependent on the footfall of the mall or transit hub you sit in, so site selection matters more than with any other format.

Café and compact QSR (₹25 – 40 Lakhs)

The most common choice for first-time investors. Beverage-led cafés carry strong gross margins because ingredient cost per cup is low and wastage is minimal. This band also gives you a seated experience, which supports longer dwell time and higher average bills than a kiosk.

Full-format restaurants (₹40 Lakhs +)

Higher revenue potential and much stronger brand presence, but also higher rent, a larger team and more complex licensing. Best suited to investors who either have prior hospitality experience or are appointing an experienced operations manager.

What to check before signing

  • Supply chain in India. An international brand is only viable here if core ingredients are locally sourced or reliably imported. Ask where the supply chain sits today.
  • Territory rights. Clarify whether you hold exclusivity for your area, and over what radius.
  • Royalty and marketing fees. Ongoing percentages matter more to long-run returns than the one-time franchise fee.
  • Vegetarian and regional menu fit. Brands that cannot adapt the menu for Indian dietary preferences struggle outside metro pockets.
  • Training and launch support. Confirm what the brand provides at opening, and for how long afterwards.

Food Franchise FAQs

What is the cheapest food franchise in India?

The lowest-investment food franchises are kiosk formats, starting around ₹15–16 Lakhs. In our portfolio that means Happi Tea, Chocolate Bash, Coffee Buddy and Seven Fries. Kiosks need less space and fewer staff, so they also open faster than full restaurants.

How much does it cost to open a café franchise in India?

Typically ₹20 Lakhs to ₹75 Lakhs depending on format. Compact formats like Viva Star Coffee start near ₹20 Lakhs; full European café experiences such as Coffeeshop Company run ₹32–75 Lakhs. That figure covers franchise fee, interiors, equipment and initial working capital.

Which food franchise is most profitable in India?

Profitability depends on location and operations more than brand. That said, beverage-led formats (coffee, tea, bubble tea) generally carry the highest gross margins — ingredient cost per unit is low and wastage is minimal. Full restaurants generate higher revenue but carry higher rent and staffing, so net margin often lands similar.

How long does it take to open a food franchise in India?

A kiosk can open in 6 to 10 weeks once the site is finalised. A full-format restaurant typically takes 3 to 6 months, covering site selection, lease, FSSAI and local licensing, fit-out, equipment and staff training.

What licences do I need for a food franchise in India?

At minimum: an FSSAI licence, a municipal trade licence, GST registration, and shop and establishment registration. On-premise dining may also require a fire safety NOC and, in some states, an eating house licence. Franchise India assists registered investors with this documentation.
