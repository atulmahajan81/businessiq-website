How Much Does a Franchise Cost in India?

Setup cost by format, the recurring fees that matter more than the entry price, and the one figure first-time investors almost always leave out.

Home › Learn › Franchise Cost

When a brand says a franchise "costs 25 Lakhs", that figure is doing a lot of hidden work. It may mean the franchise fee alone, the fee plus fit-out, or total setup excluding working capital. Those are very different numbers, and the gap between them is where budgets break.

This guide separates the four cost categories that actually determine what you need in the bank.

On the figures below: these are indicative ranges for international brands in India, drawn from the formats BusinessIQ works with. Actual costs vary significantly with city, site size, local rentals and brand specification. Treat them as a planning baseline, not a quotation.

The four cost categories

1. Franchise fee (one-time)

Paid up front to the franchisor for the right to operate under the brand. Usually covers initial training, the operations manual and launch support. This is the number brands quote most readily — and the smallest of the four for most formats.

2. Capital expenditure (one-time)

CapEx covers everything physical: fit-out, equipment, furniture, signage and IT. Typically the largest single line. Brands normally specify design standards and may require approved vendors, which protects consistency but limits how far you can economise. A site needing structural work can push CapEx well past the brochure estimate.

3. Deposits and licensing (one-time)

Commercial rent in India usually requires a security deposit of several months, sometimes substantially more in prime retail. Add registrations: GST, municipal trade licence, shop and establishment, plus FSSAI for food businesses.

4. Working capital (the one people forget)

Working capital funds operations — stock, salaries, rent, utilities — until the outlet sustains itself. It is not optional, and it is not small.

This is the most common way franchise investments fail in their first year. Not a bad brand or a bad location, but an operator who spent everything on setup and had nothing left to trade through the months before break-even. Budget several months of full operating cost on top of your setup figure.

What a mid-range budget actually looks like

A worked example for a compact café at the middle of its range, to show how the four categories distribute. Your own figures will differ, but the shape usually holds.

Note that the franchise fee is roughly 15% of the real total. A brand quoting "₹6 Lakh franchise fee" is describing about a sixth of what you will actually need.

Ongoing costs

One-time costs get the attention, but recurring fees determine long-run returns.

CostTypical basisWhat to check
Royalty% of gross salesPayable on sales, usually regardless of profit
Advertising fund% of gross salesWhether the franchisor contributes, and how spend is governed
Local marketingFixed or % commitmentOften separate from the ad fund — check for both
Technology / POSMonthly feeMandatory systems you cannot substitute
RenewalOne-time, at term endMay require refurbishment to current brand standards

Royalties outweigh the entry fee. On a ten-year term, a percentage of gross sales almost always totals more than the up-front franchise fee — often several times more. When comparing two brands, model the royalty across the full term, not just the fee on day one.

Questions to ask before you budget

  • Does the quoted figure include fit-out, or is it fee-only?
  • Is equipment included, leased, or purchased separately?
  • Are approved vendors mandatory, and how do their prices compare to market?
  • What is the royalty charged on — gross sales, net sales, or sales after discounts?
  • Is local marketing spend required in addition to the advertising fund?
  • What does renewal cost, and does it require refurbishment?
  • What working capital do existing franchisees say they actually needed?

That last question is the most useful one on the list, and the only reliable source for it is existing franchisees rather than the brand.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest franchise to start in India?

Service, coaching and practitioner models needing little or no commercial premises start from roughly ₹5 Lakhs. Kiosk and compact retail formats generally begin around ₹15 Lakhs. Low entry cost is not the same as low risk — smaller formats lean heavily on location and footfall.

What ongoing fees do franchisees pay?

Two are standard: a royalty (a percentage of gross sales, usually payable whether or not you are profitable) and an advertising fund contribution, commonly 1–3% of sales. Some agreements add separate local marketing obligations and technology fees.

How much working capital do I need?

Several months of full operating costs — rent, salaries, stock, utilities — beyond your setup budget. Outlets rarely break even immediately, and running out of working capital shortly after opening is among the most common and most avoidable causes of failure.

Is the franchise fee the total cost?

No. It is a one-time payment for brand rights, usually covering initial training and launch support. Fit-out, equipment, deposits, licensing and working capital typically total several times the fee. Always compare brands on total setup cost.
